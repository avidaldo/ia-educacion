## An assistant that works with your sources

You upload your materials; it answers from them and cites where each statement comes from.

Notas:
Gemini Notebook was called NotebookLM until July 2026; the old address redirects. Notebooks can also be used from the Gemini app.

---

<!-- .slide: id="fundamentado" class="comparison" -->
## General chatbot or notebook

### General chatbot
Answers with what it learned and with what it finds on the web.

### Notebook
Answers from the sources you give it, with citations to the exact passage.

---

<!-- .slide: id="no-infalible" class="statement" -->
## Grounded is not infallible

It can summarise badly, mix up sources or fill in with general knowledge: the citations are there to check it.

---

<!-- .slide: id="fuentes" -->
## Which sources it accepts

- PDFs, web pages and YouTube videos.
- Audio, Google Docs and Slides, pasted text.
- Photos of handwritten notes and recordings from a phone.
- Standard access: 100 notebooks, 50 sources in each.

---

<!-- .slide: id="que-genera" class="cards" -->
## What it generates

### Audio and video
Podcast-style overviews and narrated explanations.

### Study
Flashcards, quizzes, study guides, mind maps.

### Visual
Infographics and slide decks from the sources.

Notas:
**Demo (8 min): a notebook with your syllabus**. Gemini Notebook (notebook.google.com) with a test account.

1. Create a notebook and upload two or three sources for a unit (the course plan and some notes).
2. Ask something that requires cross-referencing sources: “which assessment criteria does this activity work on?”.
3. Open a citation and check that the passage says what the notebook claims.
4. Start a short audio overview and, while it is being generated, some revision flashcards.

**Expected result:** answers that fit the syllabus, with citations that can be checked.
**If it fails:** show the citations of an answer generated beforehand.
**Key message:** citations are what sets a notebook apart; open them before using a statement.

## Uses in teaching

### Preparing lessons
One notebook per subject; comparing sources; study guides you review before sharing.

### With your students
They make their own notebook to revise, and analyse whether the source says what the notebook claims.

<!-- .slide: id="moodle" -->
## What about quizzes for Moodle?

- The notebook’s quizzes cannot be exported to Moodle.
- Ask the notebook’s chat for questions in GIFT format.
- Give it a question exported from your Moodle as an example.

## Notebook or project

### Project (ChatGPT, Claude)
Producing documents, code or materials from a context.

<!-- .slide: id="practica-cuaderno" class="practice" -->
## Your first notebook

1. Create a notebook with the materials of a unit.
2. Ask three questions a student would ask.
3. Check the citations of one answer.
4. Generate revision flashcards and correct the ones that do not convince you.

**15 minutes.** Outcome: a notebook ready for a unit.
