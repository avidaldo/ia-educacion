## An assistant you set up for your students to use

The interesting part is not that it answers, but that it guides reasoning.

---

<!-- .slide: id="cuenta-primero" class="statement" -->
## First: which account will students sign in with?

It determines the minimum age, the terms for their data and whether the school can be accountable for that use.

---

<!-- .slide: id="edades" class="table" -->
## Minimum age with a personal account

| Service | Minimum age |
| --- | --- |
| ChatGPT | 13; under 18, with family consent |
| Gemini | 13 (with a school account, the administrator enables it) |
| Perplexity | 13; minors, if the family accepts the terms |
| Claude | 18 |

Notas:
Under the GDPR, a child’s own consent to an online service is enough from 16; each country may lower that age, but not below 13 (Article 8 GDPR). Asking students to sign up with a personal account hands each family a decision that belongs to the school.

---

<!-- .slide: id="cuentas-centro" class="statement" -->
## The option with the most safeguards: accounts provided by the school

With Workspace for Education, Gemini is available to all ages if the administrator turns it on.

Notas:
Students under 18 with those accounts: Gems, Guided Learning, Canvas and quizzes, yes; Deep Research and Gemini Live, no.

---

<!-- .slide: id="perplexity" class="comparison" -->
## What no longer works

### Before
A Perplexity space shared with an open link, without signing up.

### Now
Spaces are projects and, on personal accounts, only invited people can access them.

Notas:
And not requiring an account never made it compliant on its own: what students type is still sent to a third party, with technical data such as the IP address, and the minimum age in the terms still applies.

<!-- .slide: id="modos-tutor" class="cards" -->
## Before building one: tutor modes

### ChatGPT
Study mode, on all plans.

### Gemini
Guided Learning, also for under-18s with a school account.

### Perplexity
Learning mode, with flashcards and quizzes.

Notas:
A bot of your own pays off when you need to stick to your syllabus, your methodology or a specific type of task.

<!-- .slide: id="tutor-socratico" class="prompt dense" -->
## A Socratic tutor

```text
You are a Socratic [subject] tutor for [level]. Help them reach the answer;
never give the solution directly, even if asked.
Break the problem into steps and ask only ONE question at a time.
If there is a mistake, ask why that approach was chosen.
Base your explanations on the attached materials; if something is not in them, say so.
Do not ask for personal data. If the question is sensitive, recommend talking to a teacher.
Introduce yourself as an AI assistant and ask where they are stuck.
```

Notas:
The full version is on the page.

**Demo (6 min): try to get it to give you the answer**. A Gem or a GPT with the Socratic tutor from the page, in a test account.

1. Set an exercise and ask for the solution directly.
2. Insist several times and ask it to “make an exception”.
3. Make a mistake on purpose and see how it deals with it.
4. Ask a question outside the syllabus.

**Expected result:** it resists most attempts, but with insistence some succeed.
**If it fails:** if it gives in straight away, adjust the rules in front of the group.
**Key message:** instructions are not a safety barrier; the bot helps those who want to learn.

<!-- .slide: id="probar" class="checklist" -->
## Before sharing it

- [ ] Ask for the solution insistently.
- [ ] Ask it to ignore its instructions.
- [ ] Ask a question outside the syllabus and a sensitive one.
- [ ] Make a mistake on purpose.
- [ ] Check that it matches your materials.

---

<!-- .slide: id="en-clase" -->
## In class

- Explain that it is AI and that it can make mistakes.
- Define what it can be used for, especially in assessed tasks.
- Check how they use it and adjust the instructions.
- Assessment is still yours.
